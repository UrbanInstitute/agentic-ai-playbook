# Changelog

## Version 2

Version 2 makes the playbook more practical for nonprofits, government agencies, and lower-resourced organizations, while clarifying the focus on governance for agents used by people who cannot reliably verify their outputs.

### Key changes

- **Clarified the playbook's scope** by clarifying the distinction between expert and non-expert users and explaining how task length, complexity, and variety affect whether specialized governance is needed.
- **Added guidance for lower-resourced organizations**, including ways to phase requirements, prioritize the highest-value controls, seek outside support, and assess vendor-built agents.
- **Replaced the formal playbook change-management model** with a lighter-touch description of how Urban governs agent releases through leadership accountability, risk-based routing, expert review, approval workflows, and an internal agent registry.
- **Added community-engagement guidance** for agent approvals and oversight, including community advisory boards and Data Walks.
- **Broadened quality evaluation** to include domain-specific measures and the quality of an agent's process, such as tool selection, workflow execution, failure recovery, cost, and latency, while clarifying that transparency, rather than completeness in measuring all outcomes, is most important.
- **Refined security controls** by emphasizing turn-by-turn analysis and the agent's full context and prior actions, and by adding lower-resource guidance for reducing the risks of the “lethal trifecta.”
- **Strengthened transparency and human oversight** with added guidance for lower-resourced organizations and clearer expectations for observing, controlling, pausing, redirecting, overriding, or disabling agents.
- **Expanded the glossary and resources** with definitions for lower-resourced organizations, more detail on explainability for complex agents, community-engagement resources, and additional material on meaningful human oversight.
- **Updated the implementation forms and checklists** to capture domain-specific metrics, human-intervention points, ongoing monitoring, fairness testing, cybersecurity review, and long-term improvement activities.
- **Expanded the intended audience** from state and local government agencies to nonprofits and government agencies more broadly.
